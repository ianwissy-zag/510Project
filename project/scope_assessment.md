My original target was to accelerate the matrix multiplication aspects of training the GPT2-small algorithm. This has been completed in the current synthesized design using a 32x32 systolic array with BF16 multiplication and FP32 accumulation. I have also expanded my scope to target the bias addition and GELU portions of the algorithm, as all that was required to do so was to add additional pipeline stages at the output of the systolic array to perform these operations. Currently they are implemented over the course of 9 pipeline stages using Pade[3/2] polynomial division to emulate the tanh function. For m3 I am considering reducing the 32x32 systolic array down to a 16x16. The current design results in a 39x speedup for the matrix multiplication portions of the algorithm, which represent 82% of the CPU runtime. The net speedup is then ~5x, not considering the acceleration of the GELU and bias accelerations. Using a 16x16 systolic array would cut this speedup by a factor of 4, to approximately 10x. The net speedup via Amdahl's law would then be approximately 100/(18 + 8) = 3.84x, with a reduction in die size and power cost by as much as a factor of 4. I plan to synthesize both versions, then determine which is preferable based on the reports from both synthesis runs. I had a slight (~1ps) timing failure in synthesis of my 32x32 systolic implementation, but due to the relatively long synthesis time (~8hours) I am treating this as a timing success rather than running synthesis again either with more timing passes or with relaxed timing constraints.
